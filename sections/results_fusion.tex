\subsection{Image--Label Fusion}\label{sec:results-fusion}

\paragraph{Even a conservative fusion design beats the field's baseline, across held-out
sources.} \autoref{tab:relatedwork} classifies Medverse's fusion strategy as early
concatenation: image and label channels are merged before a shared U-Net processes them,
committing to a single fusion point at the very start of the network. This section asks
whether that architectural difference, independent of every other design choice this
thesis makes elsewhere, already produces a measurable difference in practice -- and does
so at the most conservative point of comparison available, and across every dataset this
thesis evaluates (\autoref{sec:results-setup}), not TotalSegmentator alone. The
single-axis control introduced below collapses image and label evidence into one token per
cell via a pixel shuffle--unshuffle operation \emph{before} the transformer; of every
variant of our architecture, it is structurally the closest to Medverse's own commitment to
early fusion. Comparing Medverse against this weaker control, rather than against the full
bi-axial model, is the more conservative claim: if even the conservative variant wins, the
win cannot be attributed to the slot axis alone. Medverse (released weights) and the
single-axis control are trained under an identical data pipeline, augmentation recipe, and
optimizer schedule, and evaluated with an identical protocol -- fixed 3\,mm single-level
crops, fp32, each model's own best checkpoint (no epoch matching across arms; Medverse
trained to full convergence, the patchset arms did not).

Rows below are grouped by each dataset's relationship to the training vocabulary, not by
how different the source otherwise looks: TotalSegmentator itself and FLARE22 both reuse
the training vocabulary and so split into a genuine
seen/unseen pair (\autoref{sec:results-setup}); MSD Prostate and MSD Hippocampus introduce
organs with no vocabulary overlap at all, so every class in that pair is unseen by
construction, not by a train/val split; HU\_LWK1's target is a HU-measurement region, not a
segmentable anatomical object, a categorically different kind of ``unseen'' from a novel
organ; TotalSegmentator's own MRI acquisition isolates a held-out \emph{modality} instead,
over classes the vocabulary already contains.

The single-axis control wins the in-vocabulary group outright -- both TotalSegmentator
splits and both FLARE22 splits -- and also wins the held-out-modality case. Medverse wins
MSD Prostate and the non-object case (HU\_LWK1); the two are close on MSD Hippocampus. Averaged across the five held-out
sources without weighting by class count, Medverse edges ahead (0.278 vs.\ 0.264) despite
losing on more rows than it wins -- generalization here is source-dependent, not a single
uniform gap. The two novel-vocabulary and the non-object rows rest on very few classes (1--2
each) and should be read as directional, not conclusive. The compute picture is the honest
opposite of a ``cheaper'' claim: the single-axis control's accuracy advantage where it holds
costs roughly 68\% more GFLOPs, with parameter count roughly matched (+4\%) and latency
essentially tied once both are measured at the same precision. Two caveats keep this from a
single-variable ablation: Medverse retains its released initialization rather than the
randomly-initialized reasoning core both patchset arms share, and does not receive the same
query-conditioning signal the reference model does during training, so this is best read as
``released baseline vs.\ our current recipe.'' \todo{Re-read once Medverse receives the same

\paragraph{Isolating the slot-axis effect, across the same sources.} Having shown that even
the conservative variant is competitive with an external baseline, this ablation asks a
narrower, fully controlled question: does keeping image and label as separate, repeatedly
cross-attending streams (the slot axis, \autoref{sec:methodology-fusion}) buy anything
\emph{on top of} an architecture that already competes, once every other confound is
removed? The reference model (\texttt{arch.dual\_axis=true}) is compared against the
single-axis control from above (\texttt{arch.dual\_axis=false}), which removes only the
slot axis; the row axis (cross-context attention) is identical in both.

Two adjustments relative to a naive dual-axis-flag toggle: (1) the single-axis control's
transformer depth is raised from $l{=}4$ to $l{=}9$ so its transformer compute is within
3\% of the reference model's -- removing the slot axis also removes a large share of the
model's compute, and parameter count and compute could not be matched simultaneously by
this route (matching compute instead leaves parameters 52\% higher for the single-axis
control); (2) both runs warm-start \emph{only} the encoder and decoder from a shared
checkpoint, with the entire in-context reasoning core (transformer, image/label embeddings,
context/query id, register tokens, cascade projection, pool projection) randomly initialized
for both arms, so neither model starts from an already-trained transformer. Both runs use
real anatomy only ($p_\text{synth}{=}0$), CT only at train time, and the same evaluation
protocol and dataset grouping as the table above.

Bi-axial attention wins every row except one: FLARE22's two unseen classes, where
single-axis is ahead (0.632 vs.\ 0.604) on a two-class sample too small to weigh heavily.
Everywhere else -- both TotalSegmentator splits, FLARE22's seen classes, both
novel-vocabulary datasets, the non-object case, and the held-out modality -- bi-axial wins,
and it wins the five-source mean (0.297 vs.\ 0.264) despite the one exception. With every
other confound this thesis can control for removed, this is the cleanest, most consistently
generalizing result in the axis. Latency is close to tied once both are measured at the same
(fp32) precision, unlike an earlier bf16 measurement of the same pair that showed a larger
gap -- that gap looks like a precision/compilation artifact rather than a real cost of the
slot axis, and is not reflected here.

\paragraph{Status.} Neither patchset arm had reached its full training budget at the point
these numbers were read, while Medverse had already converged -- so
\autoref{tab:fusion-medverse} may understate Medverse's eventual ceiling more than it
understates either patchset arm's. Both comparisons are N=1 seed per arm, and several rows
above rest on very few classes (FLARE22's unseen split, both MSD sources, HU\_LWK1) --
directional evidence, not settled effect sizes. The params mismatch (+52\% for single-axis)
also leaves a residual capacity confound in \autoref{tab:fusion-axis} even after
compute-matching.
