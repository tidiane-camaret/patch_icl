\subsection{Coarse-to-Fine Cascade}\label{sec:results-cascade}

This section asks four questions about the coarse-to-fine cascade
introduced in \autoref{sec:methodology-cascade}, in order: whether it
helps in-distribution at all; which of its mechanisms are responsible;
whether that benefit survives a shift in modality and class vocabulary;
and whether the individual mechanisms do too.

\paragraph{Coarse-to-fine cascading improves accuracy in-distribution.}
A spacing sweep from 4mm to 1.5mm crop resolution (117 classes, 3897
samples) raises macro Dice from \textbf{0.361 to 0.538} (+0.177). The
locator's containment of the target structure is already high at the
coarse level (0.922 mean, vs.\ an oracle ceiling of 0.970 -- a 0.048 gap)
and does not predict which classes gain the most at the fine level: once
a class is roughly localized, the remaining error is dominated by
segmentation quality at the fine crop, not by further localization
failure. A second, independent measurement on the \texttt{exp92}
checkpoint confirms the same monotonic pattern across a full three-level
cascade (\autoref{tab:cascade-monotonic}).

\noindent Accuracy improves monotonically from coarsest to finest level,
at a cost of 11673 GFLOPs and 666ms wall-clock for the full three-level
pass.

\paragraph{Which mechanisms drive that gain.} Having established that the
cascade helps, this subsection isolates which of its design choices are
responsible: a controlled ablation of two of the cascade mechanisms
introduced in \autoref{sec:methodology-cascade}, the query prior and the
cross-level register tokens. A three-arm chain --
\texttt{145} $\to$ \texttt{146} $\to$ \texttt{147} -- resumes the same
starting checkpoint independently for each arm and changes exactly one
setting per step, evaluated in-distribution on TotalSegmentator (119
classes, real anatomy only, $p_{\text{synth}}{=}0$) at a shared
epoch-59 cutoff (60-epoch budget).

  \centering
  \small

  macro Dice (119 classes), epoch 59.}
  \label{tab:cascade-ablation}
  \begin{tabular}{llcc}
    \toprule
    Arm & Config & Dice & $\Delta$ \\
    \midrule
    145 & GT prior (perturbed), registers off & 0.499 & -- \\
    \textbf{146} & \textbf{Predicted prior, registers off} & \textbf{0.525} & \textbf{+0.026} \\

\paragraph{A real predicted prior beats a perturbed ground-truth prior.}
Arms 145 and 146 differ only in what feeds the query prior at the fine
level: a perturbed ground-truth mask (dilated, eroded, shifted, and
noised) versus the model's own real previous-level prediction. The real
prediction wins, 0.499 $\to$ 0.525 macro Dice, improving 90 of 119
classes (median $+0.026$). This is an ablation of \emph{which} non-null
prior source is better, not of whether a prior helps at all --
\autoref{sec:results-cascade} does not yet contain an arm that drops the
query prior entirely.

\paragraph{Register carry regresses accuracy.} Arms 146 and 147 differ
only in \texttt{cascade\_registers}. Turning it on regresses macro Dice,
0.525 $\to$ 0.505, across 77 of 119 classes, and costs roughly 2.5\%
additional inference latency for it. The regression is larger on classes
held out of training than on seen classes ($-0.030$ vs.\ $-0.009$ mean
per-class Dice), and is not uniform across anatomy: the worst-hit classes
are almost all repeated fine structures that co-occur many times in one
volume -- individual ribs and vertebral levels, with
\texttt{brachiocephalic\_vein\_left} collapsing to Dice 0.0 at every
evaluated epoch once registers are on. A smaller set of large,
uniquely-shaped structures (lungs, skull, aorta) improve under registers,
consistent with the carried state helping global context at the cost of
confusing instance disambiguation among near-identical repeated
structures.

\paragraph{Generalization is modality- and quality-gated, not free.} The
in-distribution gain above does not transfer unconditionally to held-out
sources. Comparing single-level against cascaded prediction on the
\texttt{exp92} checkpoint across three of the sources in
\autoref{tab:ood-sources} (\autoref{tab:cascade-ood-headline}) shows a
consistent pattern: the cascade helps when the coarse level's own
single-level prediction is already reasonable and the modality matches
training (HU\_LWK1, CT), and hurts -- badly, in one case -- when the
coarse level is already failing (ISLES22, GNC\_705, both MRI, both
lesion targets far outside TotalSegmentator's anatomical-organ training
distribution).

\noindent An independent, indirect check on a different architecture
corroborates the same mechanism: feeding Medverse's own imperfect coarse
prediction forward as a query prior hurts its cascade universally, across
all 7 held-out sources tested, sometimes catastrophically (MSD
Hippocampus 0.691 $\to$ 0.050). Taken together, this is evidence that
compounding coarse-level error into the fine level is a property generic
to coarse-to-fine cascades, not specific to this architecture's design.

\paragraph{Neither in-distribution mechanism ablation replicates on held-out
generalization sources either.} The modality-gated pattern above is about
whether cascading at all helps or hurts; this second check asks whether
the specific mechanisms ablated above (prior source, register carry) keep
the same direction OOD. The same three checkpoints, evaluated on nine
held-out sources -- the eight of \autoref{tab:ood-sources} plus NasalSeg
(CT, sinus/nasal-cavity classes, not yet added to that table), added
2026-09-26 (each source's own cascade ladder; \texttt{msd\_hippocampus}
single-level only), give a mixed picture.

The prior-source change (145 $\to$ 146) helps 5 of 9 sources and hurts 4 --
close to an even split, notably weaker than the clear majority-positive
direction seen in-distribution (90 of 119 classes). The register-carry
change (146 $\to$ 147) helps 3 of 9 and hurts 6 -- a majority in the same
direction as its in-distribution regression, though far from uniform:
\texttt{HU\_LWK1}'s cascade does not regress under registers here
(0.152 $\to$ 0.155), and two sources gain substantially instead --
\texttt{MSD Hippocampus} ($+0.097$) and \texttt{NasalSeg} ($+0.061$).
NasalSeg is the one source where both mechanism changes move in the same
direction as their in-distribution counterparts (prior-source $+0.149$,
registers $+0.061$); FLARE22 only partially agrees, gaining from the
prior-source change ($+0.020$) but losing slightly to register carry
($-0.007$).
