\subsection{Coarse-to-Fine Cascade}\label{sec:methodology-cascade}

The cascade connects consecutive resolution levels through two
mechanisms: a query prior and region-restricted fine processing.

\paragraph{Query prior.} Medverse passes the previous step's prediction, together with the target
image, through a third U-Net branch that shares weights with its context
branch; during training, this input is a downsampled ground-truth mask
standing in for a coarse prediction. Iris has no multi-step inference
and uses no prior. At level $\ell > 1$, we instead initialize the target's
label token directly from level $\ell{-}1$'s own prediction:
%
\begin{equation}
  z^{\text{lbl}}_{\ell,0} = \mathrm{LblEmbed}\big(\mathrm{Warp}_{\ell-1 \to \ell}(\hat{L}^t_{\ell-1})\big),
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathrm{Warp}$ resamples level $\ell{-}1$'s prediction onto level
$\ell$'s crop grid, correcting for the two levels' different centers,
field of view, and any augmentation applied independently to each. This
makes the previous level's belief part of the same token stream processed
by dual attention alongside the image (\autoref{sec:methodology-fusion}),
rather than a separate side channel. The previous level's prediction is detached before warping, so no
gradient flows between levels through the prior. \autoref{fig:query-prior} shows this
flow across two levels.

\paragraph{Region-restricted fine processing.} Medverse processes the entire volume with a sliding window at every
step after the first. Once a coarse level
has localized the structure of interest, we instead restrict the sliding
window at the next, finer level to the coarse prediction's foreground
region, thresholded and padded by a fixed margin. Compute at fine levels
then scales with the size of the structure rather than the size of the
input volume, at the cost of depending on the coarse level's localization
being approximately correct.
