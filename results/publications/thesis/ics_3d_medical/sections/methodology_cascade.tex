\subsection{Coarse-to-Fine Cascade}\label{sec:methodology-cascade}

The cascade processes the target at $M$ resolution levels. Consecutive levels
are linked in two ways: the previous level's prediction initializes the
target's label tokens, and it determines which region the next level
processes.

\paragraph{Prediction as prior.}
At level $\ell > 1$, the target's label tokens are initialized from the
prediction of level $\ell{-}1$:
%
\begin{equation}
  z^{\text{lbl}}_{\ell,0} = \mathrm{LblEmbed}\big(\mathrm{Warp}_{\ell-1 \to \ell}(\hat{L}^t_{\ell-1})\big),
  \label{eq:query-prior}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathrm{Warp}$ resamples the prediction onto the crop grid of level
$\ell$, accounting for the different center, field of view, and augmentation
of the two crops. The prior thus enters the same token stream as the context
masks and is processed by the same dual attention layers, instead of through a
separate branch as in Medverse (\autoref{sec:fundamentals-relatedwork}). The
prediction is detached before warping, so no gradients flow from one level
back into the previous one. \autoref{fig:query-prior} shows the flow across
two levels.

\paragraph{Region-restricted processing.}
At each level after the first, we threshold the previous prediction, place a
box around the predicted foreground, pad it by a fixed margin, and run the
sliding window only inside this box. The cost of the fine levels then depends
on the size of the structure rather than the size of the volume. The drawback
is that fine levels cannot recover structures that the coarse level missed or
placed far from their true position.
