\section{\iflanguage{english}{Conclusion}{Fazit}}\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis studied in-context segmentation of 3D medical images and addressed
three questions: how to combine image and label information, where to spend
compute, and how to obtain training data beyond the annotated classes.

For the first question, we proposed dual attention, which keeps image and label
tokens separate and lets them interact in every layer. Trained on
TotalSegmentator CT, dual attention improved over merging the two once on 12
of 13 evaluation rows, most on new targets, and with the fewest parameters of
the compared variants. A retrained Medverse architecture, initialized from its
released weights, remained better on most datasets with new targets.

For the second question, we proposed a coarse-to-fine cascade in which each
level receives the previous level's prediction as a prior and processes only
the region that prediction localizes. The cascade was more than an order of
magnitude faster than Medverse, while the accuracy comparison depended on how
Medverse was run. Training with the model's own predictions as prior worked
better than training with perturbed ground-truth masks.

For the third question, we proposed a synthetic task generator that paints
procedural shapes onto scans. The canvas mattered more than the variety of
shapes: painting onto real scans improved three of four lesion datasets
at a single level without reducing accuracy on TotalSegmentator. Under cascade
inference, however, lesion segmentation remained largely unsolved.
