\subsection{Image--Label Fusion}\label{sec:results-fusion}

\paragraph{Isolating the slot-axis effect.} The design choice this axis
makes is keeping image and label tokens as separate streams that
repeatedly cross-attend at every layer (the slot axis,
\autoref{sec:methodology-fusion}), rather than fusing them once before the
transformer. To isolate its effect, the reference model
(\texttt{arch.dual\_axis=true}) is compared against a single-axis control
that removes the slot axis (\texttt{arch.dual\_axis=false}): image and
label evidence are merged into one token per cell via a pixel
shuffle--unshuffle operation before the transformer, closer in spirit to
Iris's fusion point (\autoref{sec:fundamentals-relatedwork}), and only the
row axis (cross-context attention) remains.

Two adjustments relative to a naive dual-axis-flag toggle: (1) the
single-axis control's transformer depth is raised from $l{=}4$ to
$l{=}9$ so its GFLOPs are within 3\% of the reference model's --
removing the slot axis also removes a large share of the model's compute,
and parameter count and compute could not be matched simultaneously by
this route (matching compute instead leaves parameters 52\% higher for
the single-axis control); (2) both runs warm-start \emph{only} the
encoder and decoder from a shared checkpoint, with the entire in-context
reasoning core (transformer, image/label embeddings, context/query id,
thinking rows, cascade projection, pool projection) randomly initialized
for both arms, so neither model starts from an already-trained
transformer. Both runs use real anatomy only ($p_\text{synth}{=}0$),
CT only, and are read at a matched epoch (160) of a shared 400-epoch
training budget.
