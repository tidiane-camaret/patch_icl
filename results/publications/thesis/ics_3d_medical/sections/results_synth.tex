\subsection{Synthetic Task Generation}\label{sec:results-synth}

On four held-out sources with no anatomical overlap with training
(ISLES22, Shifts-MS, ATLAS v2.0, GNC\_705 -- lesion targets on MRI, one
Dixon), the baseline checkpoint scores Dice 0.008--0.082. Widening the
shape generator's family mix or dosage on the synthetic-bank canvas
(\autoref{sec:methodology-synth}) left every source within noise of
baseline. The bank turned out to be built entirely from CT scans, while
every target source here is MRI -- a canvas-modality mismatch, not a
shape-diversity problem.

\paragraph{Swapping the canvas closes the gap.} A matched pair isolates
the fix: identical checkpoint, shape family (scatter\_field), dose, and
training budget, only the canvas source changes from the synthetic bank
to a real MRI subject. Every source improves except ATLAS v2.0, a
single compact chronic lesion -- the only non-multi-focal target of the
four, plausibly outside what a multiplicity-targeted family like
scatter\_field can reach.

Extending the real-host canvas from one shape family to all seven, a
wider real-subject pool (every organ, CT and MRI), and a longer training

progression -- while easing back slightly on the two brain-lesion
sources from stage 2's peak.

\paragraph{The gain survives cascading.} Resuming the stage-3 checkpoint
into coarse-to-fine cascade training (\autoref{sec:methodology-cascade})
and re-evaluating the same nine OOD sources tests whether the real-host
canvas helps under a different training and inference regime, not only
the single-level one it was found under.
