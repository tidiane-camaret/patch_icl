\subsection{Synthetic Task Generation}\label{sec:results-synth}

This section isolates the effect of the synthetic generator itself
(\autoref{sec:methodology-synth}) -- paint noise texture and
shape/intensity calibration -- from the model's training data mix.
All runs below are trained on CT only; the separate question of what
adding MRI training data does to generalization is a training-regime
choice, not a property of the synthetic generator, and is reported
elsewhere rather than folded into these numbers.

\paragraph{Texture noise beats flat noise beats real-only.} A
controlled three-arm chain shares one starting checkpoint and varies
only the paint-noise mechanism: real-only ($p_{\text{synth}}{=}0$),
synthetic with i.i.d.\ paint noise ($p_{\text{synth}}{=}0.3$), and
synthetic with multi-octave correlated texture noise (same
$p_{\text{synth}}$). Metric: TotalSegmentator validation Dice, seen and
unseen class macro splits, at a shared epoch-50 cutoff.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{Paint-noise ablation, TotalSegmentator validation Dice,
  epoch 50. Neither synthetic arm had converged at this cutoff (both
  still rising); the real-only arm had already plateaued.}
  \label{tab:synth-texture}
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    Arm & Dice & seen & unseen \\
    \midrule
    Real-only & 0.4868 & 0.5737 & 0.3886 \\
    Flat i.i.d.\ noise & 0.4899 & 0.5676 & 0.4022 \\
    \textbf{Multi-octave texture} & \textbf{0.4955} & \textbf{0.5748} & 0.4059 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Texture noise wins on all three metrics -- notably it recovers the
seen-class cost that flat noise alone pays (narrowly beating even the
real-only arm), while matching flat noise's unseen-class gain.

\paragraph{Shape and intensity realism, calibrated against measurement.}
The procedural shape-mode generator's host-anchored intensity
contrast (\autoref{sec:methodology-synth}) was calibrated against a
fresh measurement of real target-vs-surrounding-tissue contrast, taken
directly on the same held-out sources used for generalization testing
(749 cases, effect size in ring-standard-deviation units) rather than
guessed.

  the shape generator's calibration range. Sign and magnitude match
  each source's known imaging contrast pattern.}
  \label{tab:contrast-calibration}
  \begin{tabular}{llr}
    \toprule
    Source & Known pattern & Contrast (ring-$\sigma$) \\
    \midrule
    ISLES22 (DWI, acute stroke) & hyperintense & $+2.42$ \\

    MSD Hippocampus & -- & $-0.51$ \\
    ATLAS v2.0 (T1, chronic stroke) & hypointense & $-0.59$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Every source's sign and rough magnitude matches its known radiological
contrast direction, validating the measurement before it was used to
set the generator's calibration range. The measurement also caught a
design error: an initial \emph{absolute}-offset version of this
calibration was, by this same measurement, roughly 7--12$\times$ too
extreme relative to the generator's own per-class variance scale --
corrected to a ratio-of-host-variance design before any reported
checkpoint used it.

\paragraph{Not yet isolated from the training-mix change.} Two further
generator changes -- cross-class intensity/variance correlation and
widened shape diversity -- exist and have been evaluated, but only on
top of a checkpoint that concurrently switched the training data mix
from CT-only to CT+MRI. Their standalone effect, isolated from that
mix change, has not been measured.
