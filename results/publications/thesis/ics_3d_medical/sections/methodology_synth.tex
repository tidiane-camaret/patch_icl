\subsection{Synthetic Task Generation}\label{sec:methodology-synth}

During training, a fraction $p_{\text{synth}}$ of tasks is generated
synthetically instead of being sampled from the annotated cohort. In a
synthetic task, each of the $K+1$ volumes consists of an image with a
procedurally generated shape painted onto it, and the mask of that shape.

\paragraph{Shapes.}
Masks are sampled from seven procedural shape families: blob, splatter, disk,
cylinder, vessel, torus, and scatter field. The scatter field places many small
components across a region. It imitates multi-focal lesions, which few
structures in the training cohort resemble.

\paragraph{Appearance.}
The painted intensity is set relative to the surrounding tissue: it is drawn
from a range defined by the local mean and standard deviation of the image
where the shape is placed, not as an absolute value. The paint is textured with
multi-octave noise, a sum of noise fields at several spatial scales, so that
it is spatially correlated like real tissue rather than independent from voxel
to voxel. Optionally, the core and the rim of the shape receive different
intensities.

\paragraph{Canvas.}
We compare two sources for the image that shapes are painted onto. The
\emph{synthetic-bank} canvas takes an image region from a precomputed bank
built from segmented real scans. The \emph{real-host} canvas uses a real
TotalSegmentator training case, CT or MRI, directly. With the real-host
canvas, all $K+1$ volumes of a task share the same subject as their image and
differ only in the painted shapes.
