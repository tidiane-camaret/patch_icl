\subsection{Synthetic Task Generation}\label{sec:methodology-synth}

A fraction $p_{\text{synth}}$ of training tasks are drawn from a synthetic
generator instead of real context/target subjects, broadening training
beyond the anatomical classes and appearance distribution the real
cohort covers.

\paragraph{Procedural shape generator.} Each synthetic task samples a
binary target mask from one of seven procedural shape families (blob,
splatter, disk, cylinder, scatter\_field, vessel, torus) and paints it
onto a canvas image. Paint intensity is host-anchored -- drawn relative
to the canvas region's own local mean/std rather than an absolute value
-- and textured with multi-octave, spatially correlated noise (matching
real tissue's autocorrelation, unlike flat i.i.d.\ paint), with an
optional core/rim intensity blend for added heterogeneity.
\emph{scatter\_field} specifically targets lesion \emph{multiplicity}
(many small, spatially scattered components), a pattern most
anatomical structures in the real training cohort do not exhibit.

\paragraph{Canvas: synthetic bank vs.\ real subject.} Two canvas sources
are available. The \emph{synthetic-bank} canvas draws a candidate
mask/image region from a precomputed bank of segmented real scans and
paints the shape into it. The \emph{real-host} canvas instead paints the same procedural shape directly onto a real
TotalSegmentator training case (CT or MRI, any organ) , bypassing the bank entirely: every volume
in a task shares one real subject as its base image, differing only in
the painted region.
